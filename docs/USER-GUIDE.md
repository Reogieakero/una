# User Guide

This guide explains how to use DCAS (the DOrSU Guidance "Chekie" system) in plain language. It is written for the people who use the system day to day: students, counselors, faculty, the guidance head, and the administrator. It avoids code, database names, and internal jargon; the technical evidence lives in the other documents in this folder.

**Audience:** first-time and occasional users, demo presenters, and support staff.

**Where the screens are:** students use the **Chekie Student** mobile app. Counselors, faculty, the guidance head, and the administrator use the **web portal** in a browser. There is no student web portal, and there is no staff mobile app.

---

## 1. Before you start

### 1.1 What you need

| Role | Device | Account |
|---|---|---|
| Student | Android or iOS phone with the Chekie Student app | University email and a password you choose at registration |
| Counselor | Computer with a modern browser | Account created by the guidance head |
| Faculty | Computer with a modern browser | Account created by the guidance head |
| Guidance head | Computer with a modern browser | Account created by the office administrator or an existing head |
| Administrator | Computer with a modern browser | Existing administrator account |

### 1.2 How to sign in

**Students (mobile app)**

1. Open the app. The first time, you see three short welcome pages; tap **Next**, then **Get started**.
2. On **Welcome back**, enter your university email and password, then tap **Sign in**. You can also use **Sign up with Google** or **Continue with Google** if your account uses Google.
3. If you do not have an account, tap **Create student account**, fill in your full name, university email, student number, program, year level, and a password, then submit. If the app asks you to check your email, follow the link in that email before signing in.
4. Tap **Keep me signed in** only on a phone you control. On a shared phone, leave it off.

**Staff (web portal)**

1. Open the portal address given by the Guidance Office.
2. Enter your email and password on the sign-in page, or use **Continue with Google** if your account was set up that way.
3. If you forgot your password, choose **Forgot password?**, enter your email, and follow the reset link. If no email arrives, contact the Guidance Office.
4. After sign-in, the sidebar shows only the pages your role may open.

> **Screenshot: sign-in screen, mobile and web side by side**

### 1.3 If your account is deactivated

A deactivated account is signed out at the next page load and shows a message that the account is inactive. This is deliberate; contact the Guidance Office. Only the guidance head can reactivate an account.

## 2. Student guide

Students do everything in the mobile app: book, check in, attend, chat, and rate sessions.

### 2.1 The home screen

The Home tab shows, from top to bottom:

- a greeting and your **next session**, with **Reschedule** and **Cancel** buttons when the session can still be moved;
- **Book a session** when nothing is upcoming;
- the latest **Announcement** from the Guidance Office, with **See all**;
- a **How are you feeling today?** mood picker, which is a private moment on your phone and is **not saved**;
- a short breathing exercise with **Start** and **Stop**;
- quick actions for booking, checking in, chat, and sessions.

> **Screenshot: Home tab with next session and announcement**

### 2.2 How to book a session

1. Tap **Book** in the tab bar (or **Book a session** on Home).
2. Under **What's on your mind?**, choose the concern that fits best. This helps the office route your request.
3. Under **How would you like to meet?**, choose **In person** or **Online**.
4. Under **Choose a day**, pick one of the next seven days. Under **Available times**, pick a slot. Sessions last about 60 minutes.
5. If you want to be known by an alias, turn on **Book under my alias**. Your counselor will see your alias instead of your name during routine work.
6. Tap **Continue**. The app moves to the check-in screen (step 2 of 2).
7. Answer the ten questions of the stress check-in. Use **Back** if you need to change an answer, then submit.
8. After the check-in is saved, your request is created and the app opens **Chat**. Your request is **pending** until the guidance head assigns a counselor.

You can only book a time in the future, and the app will not offer a slot that is already taken. If you have not completed a recent stress check-in, the app asks you to complete one before the booking is created.

> **Screenshot: Book a session, step 1 of 2**

### 2.3 What the session statuses mean

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Pending** | Your request was received. The guidance head has not assigned a counselor yet. |
| **Assigned** | A counselor has been chosen. The counselor still needs to confirm the time. |
| **Confirmed** | The session is set. The date, time, counselor, and video link (if online) are final. |
| **Completed** | The session happened. You can rate it once. |
| **No-show** | The counselor recorded that the session did not take place. |
| **Canceled** | You or the office canceled the request or session. |

The **Sessions** tab groups these into **Upcoming**, **Past**, and **Canceled**. The app spells the tab "Canceled" with one L; that is only a label.

### 2.4 How to reschedule

1. Open **Sessions** (or the next-session card on Home).
2. Find the session and tap **Reschedule**.
3. Pick a new day and time from the available slots, then confirm.

Rules:

- You can reschedule a **pending**, **assigned**, or **confirmed** session.
- A **confirmed** session can be moved only until **24 hours before** it starts. Inside that window the app asks you to contact the Guidance Office instead.
- The new time must be in the future and inside the counselor's availability.

### 2.5 How to cancel

1. Open the session and tap **Cancel**.
2. Confirm the cancellation.

You can cancel a **pending**, **assigned**, or **confirmed** session from any of those steps. A completed, no-show, canceled, or rejected session cannot be canceled again. If the session is inside the 24-hour window and you cannot attend, contact the Guidance Office; do not simply not show up.

### 2.6 How to take the stress check-in (PSS-10)

The check-in is a ten-question stress questionnaire. It is not a diagnosis and it is not a test you can pass or fail.

There are three moments when you may see it:

1. **During booking** - it is step 2 of 2 and is required to create the request.
2. **Before a confirmed session** - the **Pre-session check-in** button appears on a confirmed session. It opens **7 days before** the session start and closes at the session start (with a few minutes of tolerance for clock differences).
3. **As a standalone check** - from the check-in screen when you are not booking, so you can record how you have been feeling.

The screen says **Private. Only your counselor sees this.** Your answers and score are never shown in notifications, chat, or analytics exports. Your counselor sees the result band, not a public scoreboard.

Score bands:

| Score | Band | Plain meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-13 | Low stress | Little perceived stress in the last month. |
| 14-26 | Moderate stress | Some perceived stress worth talking about. |
| 27-40 | High perceived stress | A high level of perceived stress; a conversation with your counselor is a good next step. |

You can retake the check-in. When a session is completed, the counselor needs a check-in submitted within the seven days before the session. If you could not complete one, the counselor can record an operational reason instead; that exception is logged.

> **Screenshot: PSS-10 check-in, question and answer scale**

### 2.7 How to join an online session

1. Open the confirmed session in **Sessions** or on the Home card.
2. The card shows that a video link is ready. Open the session details and use the join action, or open the Google Meet link from your confirmation.
3. Allow microphone and camera access in the browser or Meet app.
4. Join a few minutes early. If the link does not open, message your counselor in **Chat** or contact the Guidance Office.

Online sessions require a real `meet.google.com` link from the counselor. If the office could not create one automatically, the counselor can paste one manually, so the session still goes ahead.

### 2.8 How to chat with your counselor

1. Tap **Chat** in the tab bar.
2. Under **Your counselors**, choose the counselor you have a session or referral with. If you have no counselor yet, the screen offers **Book a session**.
3. Type in the message box at the bottom and tap send. Messages appear for both sides without refreshing.
4. A **Closed** label means the conversation has been closed by the office; you can still read the history. Ask the office to reopen a conversation if you need it.

Messages are not a crisis service. If you are in immediate danger, contact campus security or local emergency services first.

### 2.9 How to rate a session

1. Open **Sessions**, then the **Past** tab.
2. Open the completed session.
3. Under **Rate this session**, choose a rating and, if you wish, add a short comment.
4. You can rate each session once.

Your rating helps the office improve. The counselor sees feedback in the staff portal; it does not change your record.

### 2.10 Your profile and privacy settings

Open **Profile** to:

- see your name, email, student number, program, and year level;
- upload or remove a profile photo;
- switch **Share photo with my counselor** on or off;
- replay the welcome screens with **See onboarding screens**;
- **Log out**.

The photo-sharing switch is the only explicit photo-consent control in this version. The **How are you feeling today?** mood picker and the breathing exercise stay on your phone and are not stored.

> **Screenshot: Profile tab with privacy switch**

### 2.11 Notifications

The bell or **Notifications** screen shows generic messages: a session was assigned, confirmed, moved, canceled, or completed; a new chat message arrived; an announcement was published. Notifications never contain your check-in answers, scores, note text, or the content of a chat message. Tap a notification to open the related screen.

## 3. Counselor guide

Counselors use the web portal. Your sidebar groups are **Overview**, **Sessions**, **People**, and **Manage**.

### 3.1 Your dashboard

**Dashboard** is the starting point: today's sessions, the requests waiting for confirmation, the referral queue, unread messages, and the sessions that still need a note. Use it as a to-do list, then open the page that owns the item.

### 3.2 How to confirm a session

1. Open **Appointments**. The board groups requests by status.
2. Find an **assigned** request. The guidance head has already chosen you.
3. Choose **Confirm**. Pick the final date and time inside your availability.
4. If the session is **Online**, add a Google Meet link:
   - choose **Create Meet link** to let the office Google account create one, or
   - paste a `meet.google.com` link.
5. Save. The student is notified of the confirmed time and link.

You cannot confirm a time outside your availability or on top of another session. If the time was taken while you were choosing, the app asks you to pick another window.

> **Screenshot: Confirm dialog with availability, mode, and Meet link**

### 3.3 How to move or cancel a session

- **Move:** open the session and choose **Reschedule**. Pick a new time inside your availability. The student is notified with the new schedule. A managed Google Meet event is updated too.
- **Cancel:** the student can cancel from the app. If the office must cancel, use the appointment actions. A canceled session keeps its history.

### 3.4 How to complete a session and write notes

1. Open **Sessions** (calendar) or **Appointments** and open a **confirmed** session.
2. Choose **Complete**.
3. If the student has no check-in submitted within the seven days before the session, the app asks for a **waiver reason**. Write a short operational reason (for example, a walk-in or a technical problem). The reason is 10-500 characters and is logged; it must not contain clinical detail.
4. After completion, open the notes editor.
5. Choose **Freeform** or **S.O.A.P.** (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan). Write the note and save it.
6. You may attach up to five images to a note. Images are private to you and the guidance head.
7. If a follow-up is needed, set the follow-up date and time. The app can create or move a linked follow-up session when the time is inside your availability.

Rules:

- Notes are only for **completed** sessions.
- One note per session. Edit the existing note rather than creating a second.
- Only the assigned counselor writes notes. The guidance head may read for oversight but does not author them.
- Note content is never sent in notifications or audit records.

> **Screenshot: Session notes editor, S.O.A.P. layout**

### 3.5 How to see a student's record

1. Open **Students** to see the alias directory. Search by alias or filter by program and year.
2. Open a student to see the case timeline grouped by month: sessions, referrals, screenings, feedback, notes, and follow-ups.
3. Note bodies are not loaded in the bulk timeline; open a note when you need it.

The record shows the alias, not the real identity. To see a real identity, use the break-glass procedure in section 3.8.

### 3.6 How to keep your availability current

1. Open **Availability**.
2. Add a window with a weekday, start time, and end time. Repeat for each block you are available.
3. Edit or delete a window when your schedule changes.

Students can only book inside these windows, and you can only confirm or reschedule inside them. If you have no windows, the app tells you to set availability first.

### 3.7 How to work with referrals

1. Open **Referrals**.
2. A referral assigned to you can be **confirmed** into a real session. This creates or links an appointment; it does not create a duplicate referral.
3. After the session, mark the referral **resolved** with a short outcome note, or **escalate** it if it needs the head's attention.
4. Reject only with a reason; the faculty member sees the status and trail but never your clinical notes.

### 3.8 Emergency access to a real identity (break glass)

Use this only when there is a genuine safety emergency and you cannot act without the student's real identity.

1. Open **Emergency**.
2. Find the student by alias in the emergency picker. You may only choose a student on your caseload: someone with a session or referral assigned to you.
3. Write a justification of at least 20 characters. Describe the emergency, not the student's clinical history.
4. Choose **Log emergency access**. This creates a 30-minute grant.
5. A counselor reveal also needs the guidance head to review the grant first. The head can reveal without a separate review because the head is the reviewer.
6. Once reviewed and still within the 30 minutes, choose **Reveal** to see the real identity.
7. Every log, review, and reveal is recorded in the audit trail. The record includes who, when, and why, but not clinical content.

If the grant expires, log a new one. Do not share the revealed identity outside the emergency response.

> **Screenshot: Emergency access dialog with justification field**

### 3.9 Chat and feedback

- **Chat** lists student threads and staff messages. Open a thread to reply. Realtime updates the conversation and unread counts.
- **Feedback** lists ratings and comments from completed sessions. Faculty never see this page.
- **Announcements** shows posts targeted at counselors; you can read them but not publish unless you are the guidance head.

## 4. Faculty guide

Faculty use the web portal to refer a student and follow the referral's progress. You do not see counseling notes, check-in answers, or another faculty member's referrals.

### 4.1 How to refer a student

1. Open **Refer a student** in the **Manage** group.
2. Enter the student's **exact student number** and choose **Look up**. The system returns the matching student; it does not offer a browse-all list. This protects student privacy.
3. If the student has no account (for example, a walk-in), type the name and number exactly as they appear on the paper form.
4. Choose the concern and priority. Complete the case classification (at least one; if you choose **Others**, specify it).
5. Add the context in the reason field. Write facts, not diagnoses: what you observed, when, and what support you think is needed.
6. Submit. The referral goes to the guidance head for triage and assignment.

You cannot refer yourself or create an account for someone else. If the exact student number is not found, check the number with the registrar or the Guidance Office; do not guess.

> **Screenshot: Referral form with exact student-number lookup**

### 4.2 How to track a referral

1. Open **Referrals**. You see only the referrals you filed.
2. The status shows where it is: pending, assigned, acknowledged, in progress, confirmed, resolved, escalated, or rejected.
3. Open a referral to read its action trail. You see status changes and notes intended for you, never clinical session notes.

### 4.3 What faculty can and cannot see

| You can see | You cannot see |
|---|---|
| The student's name and number on the referral you filed | Counseling notes |
| The referral status and action trail | Check-in answers or scores |
| Your own referral reports | Another faculty member's referrals |
| Announcements targeted at faculty | Office-wide analytics |

### 4.4 Faculty chat

**Chat** lets you message counselors and the guidance head about a referral or a student concern. It is for coordination, not for sending clinical detail. Keep messages short and factual.

## 5. Guidance head guide

The guidance head runs the office: assignment, analytics, accounts, announcements, security review, and backups.

### 5.1 Assign or reject appointment requests

1. Open **Appointments**.
2. Find a **pending** request. Choose **Assign** and pick a counselor. The request becomes **assigned** and the counselor is notified.
3. To return an assigned request to the queue, **Unassign** it first; only then can it be rejected.
4. **Reject** a pending request with a reason. The student sees the rejected status.

You do not confirm sessions; the counselor does. You can read the full board for oversight.

### 5.2 Reports and analytics

1. Open **Reports**.
2. Choose a date range and a cohort grouping (program, year level, or college).
3. Read the sections: sessions, referrals, feedback, screenings, wellbeing trend, cohort stress, and data quality.
4. Use the filters and the export action to download the Excel workbook.
5. The **digest** is an in-app summary; it is not emailed.

Small groups are suppressed: any cell below five is hidden before display and export. This is intentional, so a small cohort cannot be re-identified from a report. If a number looks missing, it was suppressed, not lost.

> **Screenshot: Reports page with cohort filter and data-quality panel**

### 5.3 Accounts

1. Open **Users** to search, filter, and activate or deactivate accounts.
2. Open **Add staff** to create a counselor or faculty account. Staff accounts can only be created by the guidance head.
3. Deactivation takes effect at the next sign-in or page check. You cannot change your own account status.

### 5.4 Announcements

1. Open **Announcements**.
2. Write a title and body. Choose the audience: students, counselors, faculty, or everyone.
3. Optionally attach an image and set an event date if the post refers to a date.
4. **Save as draft** hides it from everyone but you. **Publish** makes it visible to the targeted audience and sends a notification.
5. Use **Unpublish** to withdraw it, or **Delete** to remove it.
6. The **Insights** tab shows counts such as published, draft, with image, and audience mix.

This is announcement management, not event registration. There is no RSVP, venue booking, capacity, or attendance tracking.

### 5.5 Security review

1. Open **Security** to see break-glass access logs.
2. Review each grant: who asked, for which alias, the justification, and when.
3. **Security events** shows the audit trail. It records that an action happened and who did it, not clinical content.
4. Review promptly. A counselor cannot reveal an identity until the head reviews the grant.

### 5.6 Backups

1. Open **Backups**.
2. **Run backup** creates a compressed, checksummed artifact in the private backup bucket and adds a row to the run history.
3. **Download** produces a short-lived link. The download is audit-logged.
4. Automatic backups run nightly. Restore is deliberately not a button; follow the restore runbook in [BACKUP_RESTORE.md](BACKUP_RESTORE.md).

### 5.7 Referral oversight

Open **Referrals** to assign a referral to a counselor, reject it with a reason, or watch the pipeline. You see the referral trail and can resolve escalated items.

## 6. Administrator guide

The administrator account is deliberately narrow. It can sign in to the web portal, open **Settings**, edit its own profile, and sign out. It cannot open the office boards, reports, security console, backups, or user management. Those belong to the **guidance head**.

If you need office-wide access, ask for a guidance head account; do not try to widen the administrator account.

## 7. Privacy in plain words

### 7.1 What is anonymous

Students are known to routine staff by an **alias**. Counselors, the head, and the office see the alias in appointment boards, session lists, chat, feedback, and reports. Your real name, email, and student number are not part of those routine screens.

### 7.2 What your counselor sees

Your counselor sees your alias, your program and year level, your appointment history, your check-in result band, your messages in your thread, and the notes they write about your sessions. Your counselor does not see your password.

### 7.3 When your identity can be revealed

Only through break-glass emergency access:

- a counselor or the guidance head logs a reason of at least 20 characters;
- the grant lasts 30 minutes;
- a counselor's reveal needs the guidance head's review first;
- the student must be on the counselor's caseload;
- every log, review, and reveal is recorded.

A database administrator or a server key can always correlate records. This system gives **pseudonymity with audited re-identification**, not mathematical anonymity or end-to-end encryption.

### 7.4 What gets logged

- Appointment status changes, assignments, reschedules, and Meet-link changes.
- Referral status changes and actions.
- Break-glass logs, reviews, and reveals.
- Announcement publish/unpublish and availability changes.
- Chat send events: who sent, in which thread, when. **Not the message text.**
- Note saves are not audited and note content is never logged.

### 7.5 What is not stored

- The mood picker choice on Home.
- The breathing exercise.
- The onboarding "seen" flag (it stays on your phone).
- The program/year list at registration (it is a fixed reference list).

### 7.6 Your controls

- Choose **Book under my alias** when you book.
- Turn **Share photo with my counselor** on or off in Profile.
- Ask the Guidance Office about access to your record.
- Use the app's **Log out** on a shared phone.

There is no self-service data export or account deletion in this version. Requests go through the Guidance Office.

## 8. When something goes wrong

| Problem | What to try | Who to contact |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot sign in | Check the email spelling, use **Forgot password?**, and confirm your email if you just registered. | Guidance Office |
| Account says deactivated | You cannot reactivate it yourself. | Guidance Office / guidance head |
| No available times when booking | The counselor may have no availability, or the next seven days are full. Try another day or contact the office. | Guidance Office |
| Booking button does nothing | Complete the check-in first; booking needs a saved check-in. | Guidance Office |
| Cannot reschedule a confirmed session | It is inside the 24-hour window. | Guidance Office |
| Video link missing | The counselor may not have added it yet. Check the confirmed session again or message the counselor. | Counselor / Guidance Office |
| Chat does not update | Check your connection; messages need a live connection. Reopen the screen. | Guidance Office |
| Report number looks blank | It may be suppressed because the group is smaller than five. | Guidance head |
| Meet link creation fails | The counselor can paste a `meet.google.com` link instead. | Counselor / IT |
| Backup download fails | The link expires after 30 minutes; run or open the backup again. | Guidance head |

If a page shows a short error with a retry action, retry once. If it keeps failing, send the page name, the time, and what you were doing to the Guidance Office. Never send passwords, check-in answers, or note text in a support message.

## 9. Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alias** | The name routine staff see instead of a student's real name. |
| **Appointment / session** | A booked counseling meeting, in person or online. |
| **Assigned** | The head chose a counselor; the counselor has not confirmed yet. |
| **Break glass** | Emergency, time-limited, logged access to a real identity. |
| **Check-in / PSS-10** | A ten-question stress questionnaire, not a diagnosis. |
| **Cohort** | A group used in reports, such as a program or year level. |
| **Digest** | An in-app summary of office activity; not emailed. |
| **Meet link** | A Google Meet video address for an online session. |
| **Pending** | A student request that has not been assigned. |
| **Referral** | A faculty member's request that a student be seen by the Guidance Office. |
| **Suppression** | Hiding report cells smaller than five people to protect privacy. |
| **Waiver** | A short, logged reason a check-in could not be completed before a session. |

## 10. Frequently asked questions

**Can I use the web portal as a student?**
No. Students use the mobile app. The web portal is for staff.

**Can I book without an alias?**
Yes. **Book under my alias** is optional. Staff still see an alias by default in routine views; the switch controls how the booking is recorded for your session.

**Is the check-in a diagnosis?**
No. It is a screening questionnaire that helps your counselor understand how you have been feeling.

**Who can read my messages?**
You and your counselor in that thread. The guidance head does not read student-counselor message bodies. Chat send events are audited without the text.

**Can my counselor see my real name?**
Not in routine screens. Only through the break-glass procedure, with a logged reason and, for a counselor, the head's review.

**Can I delete my account?**
Not from the app. Contact the Guidance Office.

**Can I get my data?**
Ask the Guidance Office; there is no self-service export in this version.

**What happens if I miss a session?**
The counselor can record a no-show. Contact the office if there was an emergency.

**Why does a report show a blank instead of zero?**
The group may be smaller than five people, so the number is suppressed for privacy.

**Are announcements the same as events with RSVP?**
No. Announcements are news posts. There is no RSVP, venue, or attendance feature.

## 11. Where to get help

- **Guidance Office, Main Campus** - account problems, booking problems, privacy requests, and emergencies during office hours.
- **Campus security / local emergency services** - immediate danger. Do not wait for an in-app reply.
- **Your counselor** - session changes and online-session links, through **Chat**.

When reporting a problem, include the role you were using, the page name, the time, and the exact message you saw. Do not include real student names, student numbers, check-in answers, or note text.
